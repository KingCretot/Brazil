Olá,

I have been part of Habitat for Humanity since I was in high school. Years later I moved into Global Village, Habitat's international build program, and it has taken me to Romania, to Ethiopia — where I built alongside President Jimmy Carter — and to India.

This November I am leading the team, and I would love for you to come with us.

We are going to Caruaru, in the sertão — the semi-arid interior of Pernambuco, in Brazil's northeast, about three hours inland from Recife. And this is not a house build. We are building cisterns — concrete rainwater tanks that give a village dependable access to clean water.

In the sertão, rain arrives hard and briefly and then does not come again for months. A household with nowhere to store it spends its days walking for whatever water it can find, of whatever quality it happens to be — and that walk usually falls to the women and the children, in the hours they would otherwise be in school. A cistern ends that. This is Habitat's Água para a Vida work — Water for Lives — and it is the change that makes every other change in a household possible.

We work seven days beside the families themselves, and beside local masons who have been building these their whole lives. We eat lunch each day in a neighbor's house. We learn a little Portuguese, badly and enthusiastically.

Thanksgiving Day is a build day. That is the honest catch of this trip — it runs straight through the holiday, and I know how much that asks of a family. It is also, I think, the reason to go. On the one day we set aside to be grateful for what fills our table, we will be mixing concrete for a tank that holds a family's drinking water through the next dry season.

The build program

Airfare is separate and is the one cost you cover yourself.
You do not have to write a $2,300 check. Habitat opens a fundraising page for you, holds and tracks the money, and gives you the payment links. Your job is to share it — most volunteers raise the full amount this way.

Age requirement: volunteers must be 17 years or older to join this particular build. There is no upper limit — there is a job on site for every pace.

Optional  ·  Before the build

Four days in Olinda & Recife

It is a long way to fly for one week, so I am arranging an optional few days beforehand — November 18–21 — based in Olinda's UNESCO World Heritage hill town, a short drive from the Recife airport. Colonial streets, working artist studios, the old market, sunset over the city from Alto da Sé.

On the Friday we are there it is the Dia da Consciência Negra, Black Consciousness Day — a national holiday, and Pernambuco is the heartland of Afro-Brazilian maracatu. The drum and dance performances that night are free, public, and not something a tour company can sell you. Then Saturday in Recife: the iron market, the frevo dance museum, and the Cais do Sertão — the museum of the very region we are about to go build in.

Roughly $280–390 per person plus airfare, entirely separate from the build cost — and entirely optional. On Saturday the 21st we join the arriving team and the Habitat week begins.
